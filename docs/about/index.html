Why mode40 started the accord

The Working Accord was started by mode40, a software company in Winnipeg, Manitoba. Its founder, Cameron Bergen, spent more than 20 years inside one of Canada's largest food processors, where he and Tracy Gromniski built the data systems the plants ran on. They have already watched software take over part of the work. The accord is what they learned, written down.

Twenty years on the plant floor

mode40 didn't start as a software company. Cameron Bergen, our founder and CEO, spent more than 20 years running operations inside one of Canada's largest primary food processors. Tracy Gromniski, now our Chief Product Officer, worked alongside him there, building the data and machine-learning systems the plants ran on, years before anyone was calling it AI.

So when people ask what happens to work when software starts doing part of it, we're not guessing. We were there.

What we saw

Some jobs went. We won't pretend otherwise.

Not everyone who left landed well, and we don't think anyone should write about this subject without saying so.

We also watched people rise.

  • Shift supervisors who used to lose the first hour of every shift to spreadsheets started spending it on the floor with their crews.
  • Maintenance stopped being the team that showed up after something broke and became the team that decided when to fix it.
  • Schedulers started asking the system harder questions than the engineers did, because they knew where the real constraints were.

The people who did best weren't the most senior or the most technical. They were the ones who were given the time and training to learn the new system, ask what it was seeing, and tell when it was wrong. Give someone that, and they go further than anyone expected, including them.

The difference between a change that lifted people and one that frightened them was rarely the software. It was whether people were told early, trained properly, and given a share of what it saved.

Why we started the accord

Most of what's said about AI and work right now comes from people selling it or people worried about it, often with good reason. We make software that uses AI, so we're not neutral, and we say so here.

But we've watched this change up close for two decades, and we've made up our minds. Cutting jobs is not a reason to use AI. A plan whose point is a smaller payroll isn't a plan. When software changes someone's job, they're owed notice, retraining and a fair share of what it saves.

So we wrote that down as ten rules, and we're putting our name on them in public, where anyone can hold us to them.

Our ask of any other employer is the same as our ask of ourselves: sign all ten rules, or don't sign. No picking the easy ones. mode40 intends to be the first employer to sign, and we'll post the date here when we do.

Our stake in this

mode40 makes software that uses AI, so we have a stake in how this goes. This site is advocacy, not research. That's why every number about jobs and wages links to its original source, and why the commitments here are ones anyone can hold us to.

One mode40 example today: AI tools draft much of our writing, and a named person approves every word that goes out, including this page.

Who's accountable. The accord is led by Cameron Bergen, mode40's founder and CEO, and Tracy Gromniski, mode40's Chief Product Officer. mode40 is responsible for every line on this site. Corrections and questions: marketing@mode40.com.

Other employers

The accord is mode40's public commitment, and it's open to any employer that wants to make the same one. There's no fee and nothing to buy. An employer signs all ten rules or doesn't sign, and is listed with the date on the signers page.

If a worker reports that a signer broke a rule, we ask the employer to answer and note on the list that a question was raised and whether they answered. We publish the count of reports every quarter and never publish the worker's name.

How this site was made

Drafted, researched and fact-checked with Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, which also sells AI. So don't take our word or its word. Every number links to the original paper on the sources page, along with who paid for each study. A person at mode40 chose and approved every line. That's rules 2 and 4, applied to ourselves.
