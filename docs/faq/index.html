Jobs and the evidence

Will AI take my job?

Probably not all of it, but it may change it a lot. Research measures which tasks AI can do, and most jobs are a mix of tasks. Jobs made mostly of tasks AI can do alone are the most exposed. The people most at risk historically are those already in a job when it changes, especially later in their careers.

The better question is what you're owed if it does change. See the worker guide.

What jobs will AI replace?

No one can say with confidence. Lists of "jobs AI will replace" are guesses. What research shows is that AI is most likely to take over tasks that are routine, text- or data-based, and don't need much judgment. In US payroll data, the clearest early effect is fewer young people being hired into the most AI-exposed jobs.9

What jobs are safe from AI?

No job is fully safe, and few will vanish outright. Work that depends on judgment, physical skill in changing settings, trust and care is the hardest to automate. In Canada, about 40% of employees are in jobs Statistics Canada does not rate as highly exposed to AI.19

How many Canadian jobs are exposed to AI?

Among Canadian employees aged 18 to 64, about 31% work in jobs highly exposed to AI with low "complementarity", meaning some of their tasks could be replaced by AI in future. Another 29% are in highly exposed jobs where AI is more likely to support the work, and about 40% are in jobs that aren't highly exposed. Statistics Canada estimated this from 2021 census data and published it in 2024. "Highly exposed" means above the median for all occupations.19

Is AI causing job losses in Canada?

There's no official Canadian count yet. The clearest early evidence is from the US, where employment of 22 to 25 year olds in the most AI-exposed jobs has fallen behind less-exposed jobs, by 19% in the latest data, mostly through less hiring.9 The researchers call it a warning sign, not proof of cause. More on the evidence.

Does new technology create more jobs than it destroys?

Over decades, yes: about 60% of US jobs in 2018 had titles that didn't exist in 1940.18 But the new jobs often go to different people, and the transition can take a generation. Read who paid last time.

Does AI make workers more productive?

Sometimes. At one large support desk, AI raised issues resolved per hour by 15%, and by about 36% for the least-skilled agents.1 But experienced programmers in a 2025 trial were 19% slower with AI while believing they were faster.3 It depends on the task, which is why people need to keep the skill to check it.

Your rights in Canada

Can my employer replace me with AI?

In most of Canada, an employer can eliminate a role for business reasons, including automation, as long as it gives the notice or pay in lieu required by employment standards law. Many workers are also owed longer "reasonable notice" under common law, and unionized workers may have technological change protections in their collective agreement. This isn't legal advice: check with your provincial employment standards office, a lawyer, or your union.

Can my employer use AI to monitor me at work in Canada?

Employers can monitor work in Canada, within limits. The federal Privacy Commissioner's guidance says workplace monitoring should be "specific, targeted and appropriate in the circumstances" (Privacy in the Workplace). Privacy rules differ by province and sector. The accord goes further: nobody is watched or ranked by software without being told first and shown what it says (rule 6).

What notice am I owed if I'm laid off in Manitoba or Ontario?

It depends on your province, how long you've worked there, and how many people are let go. Each province sets minimum notice or pay in lieu for individual layoffs, and common law often requires more. When 50 or more people are let go within four weeks, longer group notice applies: in Manitoba, 10 weeks for 50 to 100 people, 14 weeks for 101 to 299, and 18 weeks for 300 or more; in Ontario, 8 weeks for 50 to 199, 12 weeks for 200 to 499, and 16 weeks for 500 or more. Federally regulated employers must give at least 16 weeks. This isn't legal advice.

Where can I get retraining help in Canada after a layoff?

Start with Employment Insurance regular benefits if you've lost your job; in some cases you can keep receiving EI while you take approved training. The federal training page and Job Bank list programs by province, and your provincial employment office may fund retraining.

Do unions have a say in how AI is introduced?

Yes, where there's a collective agreement. Many agreements include technological change clauses, and Canadian unions are now bargaining over AI directly; Unifor has negotiated AI terms in recent agreements. The Canadian Labour Congress is calling for labour law to be updated for AI. The ten rules are written so they can be used in bargaining word for word.

For employers

What should an AI policy for the workplace include?

At minimum: which tasks the software will do and which decisions stay with people; who signs off; whether it monitors anyone; how saved time is shared; and what happens to anyone whose job changes, including notice, retraining and severance. Those are the core of the ten rules of The Working Accord. Read the employer guide.

How should an employer tell staff about AI before bringing it in?

Early, in writing, and before the decision is final. Say which tasks the software will take on, whose jobs that touches, whether it monitors anyone, and how saved time will be used. Give people a real way to raise concerns.

History

Who were the Luddites?

English textile workers who broke machines between 1811 and 1816. They weren't against technology itself; they were fighting over wages and who got the gains, at a time when unions were illegal.13 More history.

Did ATMs reduce the number of bank tellers?

Not at first. Fewer tellers worked in each branch, but banks opened many more branches, so teller jobs held for years.16 The US government now projects about 13% fewer tellers over the next decade. The full story.

What is technological unemployment?

Job loss caused by labour-saving technology arriving faster than new uses for labour. Keynes called it "a temporary phase of maladjustment" in 1930.14 Temporary can still mean years for the people living through it.

About the accord

What is The Working Accord?

A free, public set of ten rules for bringing AI into a workplace fairly: human sign-off, no secret monitoring, shared time savings, and notice, retraining and severance before any job is cut. Workers sign to say the rules are the floor; employers sign all ten as a commitment. Read the rules.

Who is behind The Working Accord?

It was started by mode40, a software company in Winnipeg, Manitoba, led by Cameron Bergen and Tracy Gromniski. mode40 makes software that uses AI, and says so on its About page. Every number on the site links to its original source. Who's behind it.

Why would a company that makes AI software write this?

Because its founder, Cameron Bergen, spent more than 20 years running operations inside one of Canada's largest primary food processors, and saw that how a change is handled decides whether people are lifted or hurt by it. The accord is voluntary and isn't a substitute for law. It's a public floor that mode40 signed first, on October 23, 2026, in writing, where anyone can check it. Any other employer can sign the same ten rules.
