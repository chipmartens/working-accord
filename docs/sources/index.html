Check our work

Every number on this site links here. For each study we list who paid for it and who supplied the AI tool, where the paper says. "Not stated" means we looked and couldn't find it. If you find a mistake, email marketing@mode40.com and we'll fix it and note the change.

Last reviewed October 2026.

  1. Brynjolfsson, E., Li, D., Raymond, L. "Generative AI at Work." Quarterly Journal of Economics 140(2), 2025. doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjae044. Earlier version: NBER w31161, 2023. Tool: built by an unnamed AI firm on OpenAI's GPT. Funding: Stanford Digital Economy Lab, whose sponsors include Google, DeepMind, Microsoft and IBM. Ties: Brynjolfsson co-founded Workhelix, which helps companies adopt AI.
  2. Lång, K. et al. "Artificial intelligence-supported screen reading versus standard double reading in the Mammography Screening with Artificial Intelligence trial (MASAI): a clinical safety analysis of a randomised, controlled, non-inferiority, single-blinded, screening accuracy study." Lancet Oncology 24(8), 2023. doi. Hernström, V. et al. "Screening performance and characteristics of breast cancer detected in the Mammography Screening with Artificial Intelligence trial (MASAI)." Lancet Digital Health 7(3), 2025. doi. Gommers, J. et al. "Interval cancer, sensitivity, and specificity comparing AI-supported mammography screening with standard double reading without AI in the MASAI study: a randomised, controlled, non-inferiority, single-blinded, population-based, screening-accuracy trial." The Lancet 407: 505 to 514, 2026. doi. Tool: Transpara by ScreenPoint Medical, which provided technical support. Funding: Swedish Cancer Society, Swedish regional cancer centres and Swedish public research funds; the funders had no role in the analysis. Declared interests: in the 2023 and 2025 papers the senior author has advised Siemens Healthineers (another imaging company) and received a lecture fee from AstraZeneca; in 2023 one co-author headed BreastScreen Norway, which has a research agreement with ScreenPoint. No author declares employment by or funding from ScreenPoint. The 2026 paper is behind a paywall and we could not read its declarations; its listed funding is the Swedish Cancer Society.
  3. Becker, J., Rush, N., Barnes, E., Rein, D. "Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity." METR, 2025. arxiv.org/abs/2507.09089. Tool: Cursor Pro, mostly with Anthropic's Claude models. Funding: not stated; METR is a nonprofit. METR now describes the result as dated for newer tools. This site was also written with a Claude model.
  4. Dell'Acqua, F. et al. "Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier." Harvard Business School Working Paper 24-013, 2023. SSRN 4573321. Tool: GPT-4 via the OpenAI API. Ties: three authors work at BCG, whose consultants were studied.
  5. Acemoglu, D., Johnson, S. "Learning from Ricardo and Thompson: Machinery and Labor in the Early Industrial Revolution, and in the Age of AI." NBER Working Paper 32416, 2024, citing G. H. Wood (1910) on handloom weavers' wages. nber.org. Funding: the authors co-direct MIT's Shaping the Future of Work Initiative, funded by the Hewlett Foundation; Acemoglu also thanks Google, Microsoft, IBM, the Sloan Foundation and others for research support. No AI tool.
  6. Allen, R. "Engels' pause: Technical change, capital accumulation, and inequality in the British industrial revolution." Explorations in Economic History 46(4), 2009. doi. Funding: not stated. No AI tool.
  7. Feigenbaum, J., Gross, D. "Answering the Call of Automation: How the Labor Market Adjusted to Mechanizing Telephone Operation." Quarterly Journal of Economics 139(3), 2024. doi. Funding: Harvard Business School. Data: AT&T archives. No AI tool.
  8. Acemoglu, D., Restrepo, P. "Tasks, Automation, and the Rise in US Wage Inequality." Econometrica 90(5), 2022. doi. Funding (working paper version) includes Google and Microsoft, alongside NSF and the Sloan Foundation.
  9. Brynjolfsson, E., Chandar, B., Chen, R. "Canaries in the Coal Mine? Six Facts about the Recent Employment Effects of Artificial Intelligence." Stanford Digital Economy Lab, August 2025, revised 2026. digitaleconomy.stanford.edu. Funding: Stanford Digital Economy Lab, whose sponsors include Google, DeepMind, Microsoft and IBM; ADP supplied the payroll data and also supports the lab. The 2026 revision measures exposure with Anthropic's Economic Index and reports the earlier 13% figure was on a measure the authors replaced; on the current measure the first release was 15%. The authors describe the findings as descriptive, not causal.
  10. Humlum, A., Vestergaard, E. "Large Language Models, Small Labor Market Effects." NBER Working Paper 33777, 2025; revised 2026 as "Still Waters, Rapid Currents: Early Labor Market Transformation under Generative AI." nber.org. Funding: University of Chicago research centres. No AI vendor.
  11. Plato. Phaedrus 275a, translated by H. N. Fowler, Loeb Classical Library, Harvard University Press, 1925. Perseus Digital Library.
  12. Trithemius, J. De laude scriptorum (In Praise of Scribes), 1492. English translation by R. Behrendt, Coronado Press, 1974. Context: N. L. Brann, "A Monastic Dilemma Posed by the Invention of Printing," Visible Language 13(2), 1979, journals.uc.edu, which notes he did not oppose printing in principle.
  13. Hobsbawm, E. "The Machine Breakers." Past & Present 1, 1952. libcom.org.
  14. Keynes, J. M. "Economic Possibilities for our Grandchildren," 1930. Reprinted in Essays in Persuasion, Macmillan, 1931. PDF.
  15. Autor, D. "Why Are There Still So Many Jobs? The History and Future of Workplace Automation." Journal of Economic Perspectives 29(3), 2015. aeaweb.org. Report: H. Bowen (chair), Technology and the American Economy, National Commission on Technology, Automation, and Economic Progress, US Government Printing Office, 1966. Full text via ERIC. Autor's journal disclosure: no financial support from interested parties for this article.
  16. Bessen, J. "Toil and Technology." Finance & Development 52(1), IMF, March 2015. imf.org. US Bureau of Labor Statistics, "Tellers," Occupational Outlook Handbook, 2025 to 2035 projections. bls.gov. Earlier figures from past editions of the Handbook: 600,500 tellers in 2008 (2010-11 edition, archived) and 545,300 in 2012 (2014-15 edition, archived). Funding: IMF publication; US government agency. No AI tool.
  17. NPR Planet Money, "Episode 606: Spreadsheets!" February 2015. npr.org. US Department of Labor, Women's Bureau, "Women's Bureau Updates," June 2020 (top ten occupations of employed women, 1980), via FRASER; current share of women in the job via Data USA (US Census data). Funding: public radio and US government data. No AI tool.
  18. Autor, D., Chin, C., Salomons, A., Seegmiller, B. "New Frontiers: The Origins and Content of New Work, 1940 to 2018." Quarterly Journal of Economics 139(3), 2024. NBER w30389. Funding includes Google.org and Schmidt Futures.
  19. Mehdi, T., Morissette, R. "Experimental Estimates of Potential Artificial Intelligence Occupational Exposure in Canada." Statistics Canada, Analytical Studies Branch Research Paper Series 11F0019M2024005, September 2024. statcan.gc.ca. Summary: The Daily, September 3, 2024. Funding: Government of Canada. No AI vendor.
  20. Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada. "Privacy in the Workplace." priv.gc.ca. Government of Canada guidance. No AI tool.
