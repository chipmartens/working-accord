If your workplace is changing

If your employer is bringing in AI, ask five questions, in writing: which tasks it takes on, who signs off on it, whether it monitors you, where saved hours go, and what happens if your role changes. In Canada you also have legal rights to notice or pay if your job ends, and free help with retraining.

Five questions to ask your employer

Ask for the answers in writing. You can also ask which of the ten rules your employer will commit to, and check whether it has signed the accord.

  1. Which tasks will the software take on, and whose jobs are they?
  2. Who signs off on what it does, and can they overrule it?
  3. Will it monitor, rate or rank me? Will I see what it says?
  4. If it saves hours, where do they go, and who decides?
  5. If my role changes or goes: how much notice, what training, what severance?

What the law already gives you

What notice am I owed if my job ends?

It depends on your province, how long you've worked there, and how many people are let go. Each province sets minimum notice or pay in lieu for individual layoffs, and common law often requires more. When 50 or more people are let go within four weeks, longer group notice applies: in Manitoba, 10 weeks for 50 to 100 people, 14 weeks for 101 to 299, and 18 weeks for 300 or more; in Ontario, 8 weeks for 50 to 199, 12 weeks for 200 to 499, and 16 weeks for 500 or more. Federally regulated employers must give at least 16 weeks. This isn't legal advice.

Can my employer monitor me with AI?

Employers can monitor work in Canada, within limits. The federal Privacy Commissioner's guidance says workplace monitoring should be "specific, targeted and appropriate in the circumstances" (Privacy in the Workplace). Privacy rules differ by province and sector. The accord goes further: nobody is watched or ranked by software without being told first and shown what it says (rule 6).

Can my employer replace me with AI?

In most of Canada, an employer can eliminate a role for business reasons, including automation, if it gives the notice or pay in lieu required by employment standards law. Many workers are owed more under common law "reasonable notice", and unionized workers may have technological change protections. Get advice for your own situation.

If you have a union

A collective agreement gives you a say. Many agreements include technological change clauses, and Canadian unions are now bargaining over AI directly; Unifor has negotiated AI terms in recent agreements. The Canadian Labour Congress is calling for labour law to be updated for AI.

Bring the five questions above to your rep. The ten rules are written so they can be used in bargaining, word for word.
